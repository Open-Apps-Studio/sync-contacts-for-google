How to merge duplicate contacts on iPhone

By Open Apps Studio · Updated October 24, 2026

To merge duplicate contacts on iPhone, open Contacts, tap View Duplicates below My Card, and review a suggested pair before merging. If you do not see that option, first check whether the copies belong to different accounts.

Use the built-in duplicate tool

Open Contacts and look for View Duplicates below My Card when iOS detects matching records. Review individual suggestions and merge the records you understand. If the suggested set is appropriate, iOS also offers Merge All.

Before a bulk operation, export the relevant list. Compare phone numbers and email addresses, not just names. Two people can share a name, household number or business switchboard.

Link cards from different accounts

Linked cards can appear as a unified contact while their underlying source accounts remain separate. This display does not mean the Google and iCloud address books have become one collection. Inspect each account’s list and web copy before deleting what looks like an extra card.

  1. Open one of the contact cards and tap Edit.
  2. Choose Link Contacts, select the other entry, then tap Link.
  3. Review the unified contact and its source cards.

Linking does not transfer ownership to one account. Apple also notes that edits to a unified contact can be copied to source accounts where the information already exists. If your goal is to keep everything in Google, follow a planned iCloud-to-Google transfer instead of assuming a linked display has completed that move.

If there is no duplicate suggestion

The absence of a suggestion does not prove that your contact data is organized as you intend. Locate a sample person in each account-specific list and compare the source records. Decide whether the copies are useful, should remain linked, or need a deliberate account migration.

Clean up the Google side too

If the duplicates exist within Google Contacts itself, review Merge & fix on the web. Google cannot merge cards across different Google accounts. Our Google duplicate guide explains how to check the result and avoid combining unrelated people.

Why duplicates can come back

Repeated imports and overlapping synchronization workflows can recreate copies. Trace the source before performing the same merge again. Record which files you imported, which accounts are visible, and which tools can write to those accounts.

A lower contact count alone is not a success measure. After merging, check that the phone numbers, email addresses and notes you need remain on the right person’s card.

Check the result before a wider cleanup

For example, suppose a personal card has a mobile number and an older work card has an office number. After merging or linking, check that each number still belongs to the intended person and has a useful label. If the cards actually belong to two colleagues with the same name, keep them separate.

Next, inspect the account you expect to keep up to date. A unified iPhone display can look tidy while the underlying accounts still contain different information. Save your pre-cleanup export until you have checked both the phone and the relevant web address book.

Review your synchronization plan

Sync Contacts for Google offers a first-sync preview and direction controls. Review the proposed matches and changes before confirming. No sync tool can guarantee that every existing or future duplicate will disappear; keep an independent backup before cleanup.
