How we compare to paid contact sync apps

If you search the App Store for Google contact sync, the top result is a paid app. It works — we won't pretend otherwise. But you should know exactly what you're paying for, and what you're granting it, before you subscribe.

The short version

The leading paid sync app charges $1.99 per week (over $100 a year) and its free tier stops at 40 contacts — enough to test, not enough to use. Our app does the same core job, free, for every contact you have. We can afford that because your contacts sync directly between your device and Google, so we don't run expensive contact-processing servers.

About that location permission

Some sync apps ask for location access not because they need to know where you are, but because iOS gives location-using apps more background runtime — it's a workaround to sync more often. It works, at the cost of granting a contacts app your movements and extra battery drain. We chose not to do that: our app uses Apple's intended mechanisms (silent push wake-ups and background refresh) and is upfront that background sync typically lands within the hour — and is instant whenever you open the app. We think that trade is right for a contacts utility; you may weigh it differently, and now you can.

Where a paid app might still suit you

Honesty cuts both ways. Paid sync apps have been around longer, some support platforms beyond Google (Exchange, Outlook.com), and location-assisted background refresh really can shave the sync delay when you're out and about. If those matter to you, a subscription may be worth it. If you want reliable iPhone ↔ Google Contacts sync without paying rent on your own address book, that's exactly what we built.

No trial that expires, no card, no 40-contact wall. Preview your first sync before anything changes, and delete the app anytime — your contacts stay in your accounts, untouched by our servers, always.
